*Updated on November 14th, 2026, after [the upper stage came back](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/spacex-plans-to-try-first-ever-catch-of-starship-rocket-from-orbit.html). This post first went up on launch night, while the flight was still under way.*

[SpaceX](https://www.spacex.com/) launched the fifteenth test flight of its [Starship](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship/) rocket on November 13th, 2026, and [called off the catch of the booster](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship) a few minutes later. The upper stage went on to orbit, and a day later the launch tower [caught it as it came back](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/spacex-plans-to-try-first-ever-catch-of-starship-rocket-from-orbit.html), which had never been done before.

## What happened at launch

Starship is a rocket in two parts: a booster called Super Heavy that lifts it off the ground, and an upper stage called Ship that goes on to orbit. [SpaceX planned this flight](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) to be the first to bring both parts back to the launch site, where arms on the launch tower would catch each one out of the air. The company wrote that success would be "the first full recovery of an orbital launch system".

The rocket lifted off from [Starbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starbase), the company's site in South Texas, at [4:47 in the afternoon, local time](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship). The [Federal Aviation Administration](https://www.faa.gov/), which licenses American launches, had [given its approval earlier the same day](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/spacex-launches-starship-in-key-test-flight-of-massive-rocket.html). The two parts separated about two and a half minutes into the flight, and the booster turned back toward the tower.

It did not get there. The booster gave up the catch and headed out over the Gulf of Mexico, [about six and a half minutes after liftoff](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship). CNBC reports that it [exploded before it reached the water](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/spacex-plans-to-try-first-ever-catch-of-starship-rocket-from-orbit.html).

The next day SpaceX gave a first account of why. "Early indications are that the booster ended its boostback early due to a low fuel mass alarm," Kate Tice of SpaceX said on the company's broadcast, as [Space.com's live updates](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-starship-flight-15-live-updates-nov-14-2026-starship-first-double-landing-attempt) record it. The boostback is the engine burn that turns the booster around and sends it home. Because that burn ended early, the booster was not where it needed to be, so its own guidance system called off the catch and steered it to the fallback spot offshore. SpaceX says it is still looking for the cause of the alarm.

This was the first try at catching the newest version of the booster. [SpaceX's page for the flight](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) says the tower caught three boosters of the first version on earlier flights, and two of those flew again.

## The upper stage in orbit

The upper stage kept going. A second engine burn, [about 26 minutes after liftoff](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship), put it in orbit about 275 kilometres up. This is the second time a Starship has reached orbit. The first was [Flight 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_14), on September 28th, 2026.

Starting 35 minutes after liftoff, it [released 26 Starlink satellites over 42 minutes](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship). [Starlink](https://www.starlink.com/) is SpaceX's satellite internet service, and these are its newest and largest satellites. [Three of them carry cameras](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) that look back at the upper stage, so that the team on the ground can inspect the tiles that protect it from the heat of coming back through the atmosphere.

SpaceX had three points at which it could bring the upper stage down early into the Pacific Ocean, and [passed up all of them](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-starship-flight-15-live-updates-nov-14-2026-starship-first-double-landing-attempt). On the morning of November 14th, Dan Huot of SpaceX said the team had looked at the pictures and found the heat tiles in good shape for the trip home.

## The catch

The upper stage [circled the Earth 14 times](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/14/spacex-plans-to-try-first-ever-catch-of-starship-rocket-from-orbit.html). Then it fired one engine for about ten seconds to slow down, fell back through the atmosphere and flew to the tower it had left the day before.

The tower's arms caught it at [about 3:20 in the afternoon, Texas time, on November 14th, 2026](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-starship-flight-15-live-updates-nov-14-2026-starship-first-double-landing-attempt), about 22 and a half hours after liftoff. "We caught a Starship, and now everything changes," Huot said on the broadcast.

## Why it matters

Until now, [no Starship upper stage had been recovered](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship). The upper stage is the harder half to bring home, because it comes back from orbit, far faster than the booster does.

This flight is still not [the first full recovery of a rocket that reached orbit](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15), which needs both parts to come home from the same flight, and the booster was lost. In the reports I have read, SpaceX has also not yet said whether this upper stage can fly again.

Starship is also [one of the Moon landers](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship) that NASA has chosen for its [Artemis programme](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/artemis/), which aims to return astronauts to the Moon.

## What to watch for

The first thing is what SpaceX says about the condition of the upper stage it caught, and whether it will fly again. The second is the cause of the booster's low fuel alarm. The third is the next flight, which could be the first to bring both parts home.
