[SpaceX](https://www.spacex.com/) launched the fifteenth test flight of its [Starship](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship/) rocket on November 13th, 2026, and [called off the catch of the booster](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship) a few minutes later, while the upper stage went on to orbit, where it is still flying as I write.

## What happened

Starship is a rocket in two parts: a booster called Super Heavy that lifts it off the ground, and an upper stage called Ship that goes on to orbit. [SpaceX planned this flight](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) to be the first to bring both parts back to the launch site, where arms on the launch tower would catch each one out of the air. The company wrote that success would be "the first full recovery of an orbital launch system".

The rocket lifted off from [Starbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starbase), the company's site in South Texas, at [4:47 in the afternoon, local time](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship). The [Federal Aviation Administration](https://www.faa.gov/), which licenses American launches, had [given its approval earlier the same day](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/spacex-launches-starship-in-key-test-flight-of-massive-rocket.html). The two parts separated about two and a half minutes into the flight, and the booster turned back toward the tower.

It did not get there. SpaceX called off the catch, and the booster [came down in the Gulf of Mexico](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship) about six and a half minutes after liftoff. CNBC reports that it [burst into flames](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/spacex-launches-starship-in-key-test-flight-of-massive-rocket.html) there. SpaceX has not yet said why the catch was called off. "We'll dig in to what caused that abort during its flight back to the tower," Dan Huot of SpaceX said on the company's broadcast, as [Space.com's live updates](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-starship-flight-15-live-updates-nov-13-2026-starship-first-double-landing-attempt) record it.

This was the first try at catching the newest version of the booster. [SpaceX's page for the flight](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) says the tower caught three boosters of the first version on earlier flights, and two of those flew again.

## The upper stage in orbit

The upper stage kept going. A second engine burn, [about 26 minutes after liftoff](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship), put it in orbit about 275 kilometres up. This is the second time a Starship has reached orbit. The first was [Flight 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_14), on September 28th, 2026.

Starting 35 minutes after liftoff, it [released 26 Starlink satellites over 42 minutes](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship). [Starlink](https://www.starlink.com/) is SpaceX's satellite internet service, and these are its newest and largest satellites. [Three of them carry cameras](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) that look back at the upper stage, so that the team on the ground can inspect the tiles that protect it from the heat of coming back through the atmosphere.

About an hour and a half into the flight, the team had its first chance to bring the upper stage down early into the Pacific Ocean, and [chose to keep flying](https://www.space.com/news/live/spacex-starship-flight-15-live-updates-nov-13-2026-starship-first-double-landing-attempt). That already makes this the longest Starship flight so far, since Flight 14 came home at about that point.

## What comes next

SpaceX plans for the upper stage to [circle the Earth about 14 times](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) over more than 22 hours. Then it fires its engines to slow down, falls back through the atmosphere and flies to the launch tower to be caught.

If everything stays healthy, the catch would come at [about 3:17 in the afternoon, Texas time, on Saturday, November 14th](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-starship-flight-15-chopsticks-double-catch-super-heavy-ship). SpaceX has two more points at which it can bring the upper stage down early in the Pacific instead, about 7 hours and about 21 hours into the flight, and a fallback spot in the Gulf of Mexico if the final approach does not look safe. [The company says](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15) it will try for the tower only if the systems needed for the return, the landing burn and the approach are healthy.

## Why it matters

No one has caught an upper stage coming back from orbit. If Saturday's catch works, it will be the first. Because the booster was lost, this flight can no longer be [the first full recovery of a rocket that reached orbit](https://www.spacex.com/launches/starship-flight-15), which needs both parts to come home from the same flight.

A rocket whose two parts both come back and fly again would cost far less per flight than one that is thrown away. The same rocket is also one of the Moon landers that NASA has chosen for its [Artemis programme](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/artemis/), which aims to return astronauts to the Moon.

## What to watch for

The first thing is whether the upper stage is caught on Saturday afternoon, or comes down in the sea. The second is SpaceX's account of why the booster catch was called off, which will say how soon the company can try again to recover both parts on one flight.
