[SpaceX](https://www.spacex.com/)'s giant rocket [Starship](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship/) reached orbit for the first time in September 2026, and in November its launch tower [caught the rocket's upper stage](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/spacex-catch-starship-rocket/) as it came home, the first time a Starship has been recovered from orbit. Neither event moved my estimate of the chance that I will one day live and work out past Mars, and nothing else in the world moved it either. Every change to that number since I started [The Belt Equation](https://beltequation.com/) in September came from checking and rethinking my own reasoning, not from the news. That is the main thing the year taught me: what would move my estimate is not a launch or a landing but later events, such as a hundred people working off Earth for pay or a business out there turning a profit, and none of those happened this year.

## Where the number stands, and why it held still

The Belt Equation breaks that one question, whether I will live and work out past Mars, into a tree of concrete events, each with a written test for when it has happened and my estimate of how likely it is. The overall chance, the headline number on the site, is worked out from those events, not chosen by hand, and today it is [about half of one percent by 2071](https://beltequation.com/), the year I reach my life expectancy.

The first time I worked that chance out from the full tree after an outside review, on September 20th, 2026, it came to [0.38 percent](https://beltequation.com/about/#story). Over the next two weeks I rebuilt parts of the tree, so that each event asks for what is needed rather than for one particular way of getting it, and then checked my first estimates one by one. The number rose and fell back, and on October 2nd it settled at 0.465 percent, where it has stayed. The site keeps a dated record of every change, called the story so far. Of its [44 entries](https://beltequation.com/about/#story), 39 are changes to the tree's shape or to my own numbers on reflection, many of them after the outside review. Three record events that happened before the tree existed, such as a passenger in his seventies flying to orbit, and two record news that was widely reported and moved nothing.

The number held still, but not because nothing happened. Every week since September 19th a reading checks the events in the tree that could happen soonest against the news, and across [nearly three hundred of those checks](https://beltequation.com/radar/) none has met a test or moved a number. The events they watch are things like one rocket flying a hundred times in a year, or a hundred people working off Earth at once, for pay, and neither of those happened. The site groups the tree's events into seven links of a chain, and the link least likely to hold is still [a reason to go, at 5.9 percent by 2071](https://beltequation.com/): [something mined or made off Earth sold at a profit](https://beltequation.com/tree/motive/M-off-earth-product-sold-at-a-profit/). Cheap rockets make such a business possible, but they do not create one.

## What happened in the world

- **Four astronauts flew around the Moon.** [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/)'s [Artemis II](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-ii/) launched on April 1st, 2026 and splashed down on April 10th, the [first crew to go beyond low orbit since Apollo 17 in 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_II). It flew before I built the tree, so my estimates started from it.
- **Starship flew four times.** [Flight 14 reached orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Starship_launches) on September 28th. [Flight 15's upper stage was caught](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/spacex-catch-starship-rocket/) on November 14th after a day in orbit, while its booster was lost over the Gulf of Mexico. The event in the tree that the catch bears on, both halves of the rocket back from one flight and ready to fly again, [already had a 95 percent chance by 2071](https://beltequation.com/tree/launch/L-fully-reusable-heavy-launcher-recovers-both-stages/) in my estimate, and it has not happened yet, as I set out in [the post on the catch](https://beltequation.com/news/2026-11-13-starship-flight-15-reaches-orbit-booster-catch-called-off/).
- **Plans slipped.** On February 27th, 2026 NASA turned [Artemis III](https://www.nasa.gov/mission/artemis-iii/) from a Moon landing into [a test in Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_III). [Haven-1](https://www.vastspace.com/haven-1), which [Vast](https://www.vastspace.com/) means to be the first commercial space station, [moved from late 2026 to 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haven-1). And China [postponed](https://www.news4jax.com/tech/2026/08/23/china-abruptly-postpones-ambitious-change-7-lunar-mission-launch/) [Chang'e 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang%27e_7), its mission to look for ice at the Moon's south pole, in August, with no new date.
- **Spacecraft reached the small bodies.** China's [Tianwen-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-2) arrived at the small asteroid [Kamoʻoalewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/469219_Kamo%CA%BBoalewa) on July 4th, 2026 to collect a sample. The [European Space Agency](https://www.esa.int/)'s [Hera](https://www.esa.int/missions/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera) [arrived on November 11th](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/hera-spacecraft-in-rectangular-orbit-around-target-asteroids) at the pair of asteroids that includes the one NASA struck in 2022, as [I wrote at the time](https://beltequation.com/news/2026-11-14-hera-arrives-at-the-asteroid-nasa-struck/). NASA's [Lucy](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lucy/) became [the first spacecraft to enter](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/lucy/2026/10/28/nasas-lucy-reaches-trojan-asteroid-region-5-years-after-launch/) the [Trojan asteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_trojan), which share Jupiter's orbit around the Sun. And Japan's [MMX](https://www.mmx.jaxa.jp/en/) [launched on October 20th](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2026/10/20261020-1_e.html), Japan time, to bring back a sample of the Martian moon [Phobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phobos_%28moon%29).
- **The map of the solar system filled in.** The [Rubin Observatory](https://rubinobservatory.org/) found [its first likely dwarf planet](https://beltequation.com/news/2026-10-27-rubin-first-dwarf-planet-candidate/), and ESA's [Euclid](https://www.esa.int/missions/about-us/directorates/science/euclid) telescope found [its first new comet](https://beltequation.com/news/2026-11-09-euclid-first-new-comet/).

Apart from Starship's flights, none of these bears on an event in the tree. The tree asks what it would take for people to make a living past Mars, and this year the world mostly showed what it takes to get there and look.

## Coming up in 2027

- **January 3rd: my yearly review.** Once a year I estimate every event in the tree again and work the overall chance out afresh, and the first of these reviews is due on January 3rd, 2027. It will settle three questions I have been holding for it. First, whether the outcome my headline number measures, industry reaching past Mars, should count material mined from asteroids as well as from the Moon. As the tree stands, that outcome requires material from the Moon, so asteroid mining alone could not bring that outcome about. Second, whether a business that sells computing from equipment built off Earth should count as industry out there, together with a new event in the tree for making working solar cells from off-Earth material. Third, a fresh estimate for [a year of living mostly on food made on site](https://beltequation.com/tree/biology/B-closed-loop-life-support-year-off-earth/), whose test I widened in September. The review also gives my estimates their first score, a check of what I said against the events that have happened since I said it. What it finds, and whether the number moves, will get a post of its own.
- **Early 2027: the first Starship upper stage to fly twice, and the first refuelling in orbit.** [Gwynne Shotwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynne_Shotwell), SpaceX's president, said in October that the company aims to ["refly the second stage by early next year"](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/20/spacex-spcx-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/) and to attempt refuelling in orbit in the same window, and [Elon Musk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elon_Musk) said on the same call that he expects weekly flights in the first half of the year. Starship's dates have slipped before. The first try at catching its upper stage [was planned for August](https://beltequation.com/tree/launch/L-fully-reusable-heavy-launcher-recovers-both-stages/) and came in November, and its uncrewed Moon landing, [once planned for 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_HLS), has been delayed. A flight that brings both halves home, with SpaceX confirming both can fly again, would meet the event in the tree about the rocket coming home whole. A first refuelling is far short of the [hundred tonnes of fuel moved between spacecraft that the tree asks for](https://beltequation.com/tree/launch/L-orbital-propellant-transfer-at-scale/), but it has to come first.
- **Some time in 2027: Haven-1 and Artemis III.** Vast is targeting a [Haven-1 launch in 2027](https://www.vastspace.com/haven-1), and the date has slipped once already. Artemis III, in which a crew docks with one or both of the Moon landers in Earth orbit, is set for [no earlier than June 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_III). Neither would settle an event in the tree, but both test what a working presence beyond Earth needs.
- **April and November: Tianwen-2 heads home.** It is due to [leave Kamoʻoalewa on April 24th, 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-2) and to drop its sample capsule to Earth on November 29th.
- **August and September: three arrivals.** Lucy makes its first flyby of a Trojan asteroid, [Eurybates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3548_Eurybates), [on August 11th](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/lucy/). NASA's twin [ESCAPADE](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/escapade/) spacecraft are due at Mars [in September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESCAPADE), and MMX in [the second half of the year](https://www.dlr.de/en/latest/news/2026/off-to-the-moons-of-mars-mmx-mission-and-idefix-rover-ready-for-launch).
- **Some time in 2027: an asteroid-mining company tries again.** [AstroForge](https://www.astroforge.com/), which lost its spacecraft Odin [in 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AstroForge), says its next one, [DeepSpace-2](https://www.astroforge.com/deepspace-2), will fly out to a metal-rich asteroid and photograph it. Its own page gives both the end of 2026 and 2027 for the launch. DeepSpace-2 would not settle any event in the tree, but it is a company going out to look at the kind of rock that industry past Mars would mine.
