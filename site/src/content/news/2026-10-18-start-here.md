The Belt Equation asks one question: what is the probability that, before I die, I live and work in a solar system where human industry reaches beyond Earth and Mars? I want to leave the planet before I die, and I would rather know my real odds than keep the wish vague. So I wrote down the things that would have to happen first, gave each a test of what would count and a probability, and let the tree work out the answer. A number can be argued with step by step, and every January the steps that were settled during the year are scored against what actually happened.

This is the start of the site's news section. When the weekly reading finds something that moves the tree, or big news that should have moved it and did not, I will write it up here beside the record it explains. A quiet week gets no post. The longer story of why I built the project is in [the essay that introduced it](https://sentient-horizons.com/essays/the-belt-equation/).

## Where the numbers stand

As of the run of 11 October 2026, the headline is 0.5 percent: the chance that, by 2071, I am working off Earth on rotation, which means months at a time living and working at an off-world site. 2071 is the year I reach my life expectancy, at about 85. Beside it sits a nearer number, 9.3 percent, the chance that I work a rotation at a station or a lunar base whether or not industry ever goes further. The bar the question sets, human industry beyond Earth and Mars with or without me, comes out at 0.8 percent ([the headline file](/data/headline.json)).

A longer life would raise those odds, and [a dial on the home page](/) shows by how much: 1.2 percent if medicine adds about a decade, 3.8 percent if I make it to 109, 10.8 percent with a 150-year life, and 14.8 percent if ageing is solved outright and the calendar stops mattering.

The headline is small because seven things all have to go right, and multiplying seven maybes gives a small number even when no single link looks hopeless. By 2071 the seven stand at 14 percent for cheap launch, 75 percent for power out there, 6.8 percent for ships fast enough to make the trip routine, 9.7 percent for bodies that hold up, 5.9 percent for a reason to go, 21 percent for governments that do not wreck it, and 9.3 percent for a seat for me. Behind the seven are [81 smaller steps](/tree/), each with its own test, and because they lean on each other the headline is worked out from the whole tree.

## How to follow it

Every Sunday evening, Pacific time, a fresh session searches for what the world did against each step's test and writes down what it found, whether or not anything moved. It may change a number by a small amount on a public source, or mark a step as having happened when two independent records agree; anything bigger it leaves for me. Most weeks it reads the 17 steps that could be decided within about five years, and the first reading of each month reads the whole tree ([the Radar](/radar/)).

Each week's reading is on [the Radar](https://beltequation.com/radar/). [The Story](https://beltequation.com/story/) keeps every change to the number with its cause, [Next](https://beltequation.com/next/) lists the ten steps that would move it most, and [Your Odds](https://beltequation.com/run/) runs the tree against your own birth year. [The Method](https://beltequation.com/method/) explains how the numbers are worked out and who did what. If you think a number, a step or a test is wrong, [the Feedback page](https://beltequation.com/feedback/) has a form for each, and I rule on every one in public. [The feed](/news/feed.xml) carries every post in this section.

## The most interesting news so far

[The first run](/story/), on 19 September 2026, came out at zero by 2071. Read against common sense, that result failed, and the fault turned out to be in the method: the calculation treated every step as an independent roll of the dice, when the steps lean on each other. Fixing that was one of four rulings I made that day, and the second run came out at 0.9 percent.

The next day the whole tree went to outside reviewers with a fixed brief: name a missing step, challenge a test, argue a number up or down. I ruled on [105 points they raised](/story/), and on 20 September 2026 the headline fell from 0.9 percent to 0.4 percent. Most of the fall came from seven new requirements on the chain.

On 25 September 2026 [I changed how every step is written](/story/#2026-09-25-food-made-on-site-counts-however-it-is-made), so that it names the thing it needs and not one way of getting it: lunar mining now waits on power through the lunar night from any source, where it used to wait on a nuclear reactor. The headline rose from 0.47 to 0.59 percent, about the width of the noise.

On 28 September 2026 SpaceX's rocket [Starship](https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship/) reached orbit for the first time ([the story](/story/#2026-10-04-starship-reaches-orbit-no-recovery)). Neither stage was caught, so the launch steps stayed where they were and the number held still. That is the tree doing its job: famous news that does not meet a step's test moves nothing.

On 6 October 2026 OpenAI posted [372 families of machine-made mathematics results](https://github.com/openai/math) ([the story](/story/#2026-10-11-openai-math-batch-not-peer-reviewed)), which bear on a line I track beside the equation and never multiply into it: a non-human mind producing knowledge beyond us. None was peer reviewed yet, so that line stayed where it was.

Since the review landed on 20 September the headline has stayed near half a percent, and in [six weekly readings](/radar/) no number has moved.

## What to watch for

The nearest event is Starship's [Flight 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_15), which [the reading of 11 October 2026](/radar/) said would make the first attempt to catch the ship. SpaceX has announced no date; that reading had it no earlier than 19 October, and the latest planning date I can find is no earlier than 8 November 2026. The step it bears on counts only when a launcher's two stages are both retrieved intact from one orbital flight and the operator confirms they can fly again, and even then it is worth about 0.14 points on the 2080 figure, because the tree already expects it.

The steps that would move the number most are slower and have no date. At the top of [the Next list](/next/) is a space traffic authority among the major powers that can enforce rules on manoeuvres and launch windows, worth about 0.27 points on the 2080 figure, a rise of about a fifth. After it come shielding that halves a crew's radiation dose in deep space, and a tonne of propellant made from off-Earth material fired in a rocket engine.

[The readings](/radar/) are also following two dated plans: Vast's [Haven-1](https://www.vastspace.com/haven-1), a small commercial space station, now launches no earlier than the first quarter of 2027, and NASA's [Space Reactor-1 Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Reactor-1_Freedom), a spacecraft with a reactor making more than 20 kilowatts, is proposed for December 2028. A plan is not an event, and the tree moves only on events.

On [the site's own calendar](/method/), the next whole-tree reading is on 1 November 2026, and the first annual review, when every number is re-estimated, is on 3 January 2027.
