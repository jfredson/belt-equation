Not to scale. What this map shows, and where its numbers come from

The Sun and the planets are drawn hundreds of times larger than they really are compared with the distances between them. At true scale Earth would be smaller than a single pixel on this page, so the sizes are exaggerated to be visible, and only the distances and the speeds are to scale with each other.

Each planet's distance from the Sun, its size, and how long it takes to go round once are from the NASA planetary fact sheet. Orbits are drawn as circles at each planet's average distance, in one flat plane; the real orbits are slightly oval and slightly tilted. Starting positions are arbitrary, so this is not the sky on any particular date.

The belt is drawn as a few thousand points between 2.06 and 3.27 times Earth's distance from the Sun, the region where most asteroids orbit (Asteroid belt, Wikipedia). The points are placeholders for a crowd of rocks, not real asteroids, and the real belt is mostly empty space.
