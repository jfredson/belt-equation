## The review

**What the step says.** The TimeAssembler roadmap holds it as: *Website step 27: "run it for yourself", a page where a reader sets their own age, lifespan scenario and path and sees the tree's number for them.* It exists only in TimeAssembler. It is not in docs/roadmap.md or docs/website-plan.md, so the first thing the build does is write it down in the repo.

**The number collides.** Roadmap step 27 is already the outside-model review (Phase 3). The ledger plan took 28 to 33 and the scan plan 34 to 38, so the next free number is 39. Recommendation: call this website step 39 in the repo and rename the TimeAssembler task to match, so "step 27" means one thing.

**Three design questions the one-line task leaves open.** Each is a judgment call, and the brief below asks Claude Code to present them with confidence, standard-versus-judgment, and the strongest alternative, per the 2026-09-08 ground rule, before John accepts them. My recommendations:

1. *A reader's age.* The tree has no year axis, only five scenarios keyed to John's own windows (2071, 2080, 2095, 2136, open), and every probability is stored per scenario. Those windows are ages 85, 94, 109 and 150 for someone born January 1986. So the cleanest move is: the reader gives a birth year and picks the same five scenarios, and the page shifts the deadline by (reader's birth year minus 1986). Baseline for a reader born in 2000 is 2085, not 2071. This needs node probabilities at years the tree has never been asked about, which means interpolating each node's probability between neighbouring scenario years on the log-odds scale (the same scale the world draw already uses), and treating "open" as the limit. That is a methodology extension and belongs in docs/methodology.md with the reasoning, not buried in a script. Alternative: snap the reader to the nearest of John's five windows, which needs no new method but makes a reader born in 2000 share John's 2071 number, which the page would then have to explain away. Recommendation: interpolate.

2. *A reader's path.* The access branch is John's: his current training, the six-year fork of his current contract, and the route from his current career into commercial crew training. None of that is a reader's path. A reader's number in version one should be the world part from the tree (does human industry reach beyond Earth and Mars by their deadline, does a seat sell for under a million dollars, do off-Earth rotation roles exist) times one number the reader supplies for the personal part (the chance they personally get the seat or the role, given that it exists), shown as two separate figures so nobody mistakes the reader's guess for the tree's output. Three routes on the menu: buy a seat, work a rotation, contribute from Earth (A0, which needs no personal number). Alternative: no personal number at all, the page shows only the world tiers at the reader's deadline. That is more honest and less interesting; the world-tier view should exist on the page either way as the first thing shown. Recommendation: the routes with one reader-set personal probability, defaulted low and labelled as the reader's own.

3. *Where the computation runs.* The site is static and the compute script is Python. Website-plan decision 3 says the site never duplicates the numbers logic. Two ways to honour that: (a) export.py precomputes a grid (deadline years 2035 to 2140 in five-year steps plus open, times the routes) into site/src/data/, and the page interpolates tier rates between grid years; or (b) port `simulate()` to the browser (about sixty lines, tree.json already carries dependencies, tiers and per-scenario probabilities). Recommendation: (a), the precomputed grid, because it keeps Python the only place numbers come from and every figure the page can show is reproducible from a committed export. The cost is export time (roughly 22 years times 3 routes at 5,000 play-throughs, a minute or two) and a coarser answer between grid points. (b) is the right version two if the page ever wants sliders on individual nodes.

**What the page is not.** It does not change any stored probability, any snapshot, or the headline. It reads the tree and shows a reader what the tree says about a different deadline and a generic route. The ledger and the Radar are untouched.

**Gates.** Per John's 2026-09-19 ruling on execution gates: the design decisions and the methodology text are the human gate, so they land as a plan document and a pull request that John reads before merge; the build itself proceeds on a branch without waiting. A new public page is a launch, so nothing deploys until the PR merges.
