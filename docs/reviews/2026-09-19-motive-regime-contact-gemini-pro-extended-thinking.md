The tree currently treats off-Earth economics as a matter of supply, ownership rules, and willing buyers. Large-scale capital deployment requires risk management and physical security. Without mutually recognised exclusion zones, companies cannot secure a physical mining site against interference. Without commercial underwriting, investors carry the full unmitigated risk of hardware loss, freezing the capital required to build a profitable mine. Furthermore, expanding to tens of thousands of people in transit (Tier 3) requires a mandatory traffic management regime to prevent catastrophic collisions, a separate structural requirement from the current tree's mere prohibition of deliberate ASAT debris tests.

**`M-power-beamed-from-orbit-to-ground-at-kilowatt-scale`**
Beaming a received kilowatt requires a massive orbital aperture to overcome the diffraction limit, or an impractically large ground rectenna for a mere demonstration. Space solar power faces devastating economic competition from ground-based solar paired with advancing battery storage. The funding for an orbital transmitter large enough to land a full kilowatt on Earth will likely never materialize. Agencies will continue to fund milliwatt physics demonstrations (like Caltech's 2023 MAPLE experiment), but the kilowatt scale falls into an economic dead zone: too expensive for a basic science experiment, yet too small to return commercial power revenue.
*Confidence: High.*

**`C3` (An intelligence not descended from us is detected)**
The node requires finding a technosignature, an artefact, or evidence of life *more complex than microbes*. Atmospheric biosignatures from exoplanets cannot definitively distinguish between microbial mats and complex flora. Resolving this node therefore relies almost entirely on detecting artificial signals or engineering. Given the vast spatial and temporal gulfs of the cosmos and the complete silence of the past sixty years of radio astronomy, the chance that a transmitting civilization overlaps with our specific observational window in the next century is negligible. The baseline probabilities vastly overestimate the likelihood of a positive technosignature detection.
*Confidence: Medium.*

**`C4` (Communion)**
These numbers are conditional on a positive detection (C3), but a technosignature is overwhelmingly likely to be incidental—such as industrial atmospheric pollution, waste heat from mega-structures, or planetary radar—rather than a deliberate interstellar message. Even if a message is received, decoding a high-bandwidth transmission from a non-human intelligence with zero shared cultural or biological referents presents a cryptographic hurdle that may be fundamentally unsolvable. Assuming a 10 to 40 percent chance that a detection translates into a deciphered body of knowledge ignores the reality that evidence of existence does not equal communication.
*Confidence: High.*
