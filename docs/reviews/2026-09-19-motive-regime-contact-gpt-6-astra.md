**Part 1 — Missing nodes**

| Missing event | Proposed resolution criterion | What it should gate |
|---|---|---|
| Sustained population at each tier’s scale | Public operator records establish continuous habitation for one year with at least 100, 1,000, 10,000, or 1,000,000 people off Earth, respectively. | T1–T4, using separate thresholds of the same measure; strengthen the existing hundred-worker node for T1. |
| A child grows up off Earth | Public demographic records and independently reviewed medical reporting document someone born off Earth reaching age 18 without having lived on Earth. | T2, alongside the existing birth node, which currently appears only under T4. |
| Local food supplies a meaningful share of consumption | Independently reviewed production and consumption records show an inhabited site supplying at least 20% of its residents’ annual dietary energy from food grown there for two consecutive years. | T2, alongside the air-and-water requirements. |

These are gaps in the supplied dependency lists; I cannot establish whether the unseen nodes cover them. The thresholds are my proposals. “Closed-loop life support” might include food, but its title alone does not establish that.

The population gap is substantial: the listed requirements can all resolve while populations remain far below the tiers’ descriptions. One day with 100 paid workers does not establish a permanent outpost, and none of the supplied requirements establishes thousands or millions of residents.

**The largest structural error is making T3 necessary for A2.** A person can work months-long rotations at an Earth-orbiting station without a profitable mine, nuclear propulsion, children raised off Earth, or a settlement beyond the Earth–Moon system. Remove T3 as a mandatory prerequisite for the stated headline, or rename the headline to say that it requires participation in a T3 civilisation. Likewise, A3 need not require a previous period of rotational employment: direct migration is another route.

T1 also needs alternative routes. Its definition permits an orbital station, but its requirements insist on a reusable lunar lander, lunar fission, and a politically durable lunar program. Those are requirements for one possible development path, not for every qualifying outpost.

**Part 2 — Criteria**

| Node | Problem | Proposed fix |
|---|---|---|
| M — Paying customer for orbital work | A contract award is not earned revenue; “commercial customer” in the title disappears from the criterion; nominal dollars make the threshold shrink over time. | Require a public financial statement confirming at least $10 million in 2026 dollars of revenue earned in one financial year from completed qualifying orbital services for an identified unaffiliated private customer, excluding grants, deposits, and undelivered contract value. |
| M — Kilowatt beamed to Earth | No duration is specified, and a brief peak says little about a useful supply. | Require at least 1 kW of net electrical output attributable to the orbital transmission at the ground receiver, sustained for ten continuous minutes and documented with calibrated measurements. |
| M — Hundred paid workers | A crew-change overlap or short flight can qualify; crew records may establish presence without establishing employment. | Require at least 100 paid workers continuously off Earth for one year, with public crew and employment attestations excluding passengers whose only paid activity is incidental to their visit. |
| M — Off-Earth product sold at a profit | “Full cost” is undefined, and one profitable subsidised transaction does not establish the claimed independent economy. | Require an independently audited product account specifying development and capital-cost allocation, transport, operations, financing, and public support, and either include that support in the economic cost or narrow the interpretation to supplier profitability. |
| R — Lunar program survives governments | Government changes are not comparable across political systems; the fixed 2026 baseline conflicts with first-flight timing; restart rules obscure continuity. | Start at the first crewed lunar flight, define qualifying leadership transitions for each country in advance, require annual funding and a crewed lunar flight under each of two successor leaders, and restart the count after cancellation. |
| R — Recovery after recession | Historical recessions count, the budget basket is unspecified, and “within ten percent” can perversely exclude growth above 110%. | Define recovery as at least 90% of the last complete pre-recession year’s level, fix the budget basket, inflation index, and provider-selection rule, and either resolve the historical milestone or explicitly restrict a replacement node to future recessions. |
| R — Shared resource rights | “Same rules” could mean identical statutes or merely compatible ownership rights, which are very different tests. | Specify the rights that must be mutually recognised—ownership after extraction, transfer, and dispute resolution—and require enacted instruments explicitly recognising qualifying rights from the other named jurisdictions. |
| R — Permanent residents | “No scheduled return” can describe stranded crew or an open-ended work assignment. | Require published permission for voluntary indefinite residence and evidence that someone has exercised it for three years without a mandatory rotation date, rather than remaining because departure was delayed or impossible. |
| C1 — Machine-produced knowledge | The wording admits existing automated proofs and does not establish either a “mind” or a result humans could never produce. | Resolve the broad machine-result criterion using the 2016 example below, or replace it with a separately forecast requirement for a machine-originated conceptual advance and documented evidence of that origin. |
| C2 — Humans learn | Textbook inclusion need not demonstrate understanding; field boundaries and “originated” are unspecified. | Require documented machine origin of a previously unpublished method, independent human explanations of why it works, and substantive teaching in two independently authored graduate textbooks within a field defined before adjudication. |
| C3 — Non-descended intelligence | Complex life need not be intelligent, and “non-human origin” does not exclude human-built machines. | Restrict C3 to independently confirmed evidence of technology originating beyond the human technological lineage, and track non-technological extraterrestrial life separately. |
| C4 — Communion | Absolute exclusion of every human-built source is not an attainable evidential standard, while “body of knowledge” has no minimum content. | Require independently reproducible decoding of at least one substantive, verifiable claim from a source established by two independent physical observations to lie beyond plausible human technological reach, with public evidence addressing spoofing and contamination. |

I looked up the following evidence; the resolution judgments are mine.

**C1 has already resolved under its broad wording.** Heule, Kullmann, and Marek’s SAT 2016 paper reports a machine-produced and machine-verified solution to the longstanding Boolean Pythagorean triples problem. The proof is approximately 200 terabytes, and the authors provide verification materials. This fits a previously unattained mathematical result whose full derivation cannot practically be checked by a human reader. It does not prove that humans could never discover a shorter proof, but the written criterion does not require that. [Research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.00723), [authors’ verification archive](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~marijn/ptn/).

**The recession node has already passed under the intended recovery interpretation.** NBER dates the recession’s end to April 2020. Using NASA Exploration plus Space Operations as the budget basket, the Congressional Research Service reports approximately $9.685 billion in FY2019 and $10.505 billion in FY2021 budget authority. Adjusting with annual CPI gives roughly 102% of the 2019 level. This is an explicit operational interpretation of the unspecified budget basket. [NBER dates](https://www.nber.org/news/business-cycle-dating-committee-announcement-july-19-2021), [CRS budget tables](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R43419/R43419.85.pdf), [BLS inflation data](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/factsheets/purchasing-power-constant-dollars.htm).

SpaceX’s historical launch chart records 13 Falcon-family launches in 2019 and 31 in 2021, comfortably above a 90% recovery floor. A literal ±10% interval would reject that growth, which is why the wording must be repaired before declaring an unqualified resolution. [SpaceX prospectus, historical launch chart](https://content.spacex.com/cms-assets/FINAL_Documents%20and%20Updates/SpaceX%20-%20EU%20Prospectus%20%28Approved%20by%20Bafin%29%20-%20June%205%2C%202026.pdf).

Two further dependency corrections matter:

- **Profit does not logically require lunar material or US–China agreement.** An orbital manufacturer using Earth-supplied inputs could satisfy the product criterion. Make lunar extraction one route, and treat international agreement as an influence unless the product criterion specifically requires it.
- **An anti-satellite testing treaty is a diplomatic milestone, not a guarantee of usable orbits.** Its criterion is reasonably clear, but it neither establishes protection against wartime attacks nor controls accidental debris. Conversely, T4 could exist without that particular treaty. Remove it as a mandatory T4 requirement.

These are my judgments, not numbers taken from sources. Except for the explicitly identified recession interpretation, they price the **existing criteria and dependencies**, rather than the stronger replacements proposed above.

I interpret conditioning as the dependencies having resolved by the same cutoff. Their completion dates still matter: biological feasibility established in 2070 leaves insufficient time for three years of residence by 2071. The tree should model those dates, or state how it averages over them; “already resolved” alone does not settle this.

**Hundred paid workers — lower.** Given heavy-launch cadence and recession recovery, transportation capacity and one financial stress test are available; demand for 100 people is not. Orbital computing, manufacturing, and mining could expand while requiring very few workers in space. Government programs can supply the missing demand, but the dependencies do not establish their staffing ambitions or habitat capacity. I allow for the weak one-day threshold in these numbers: even that threshold requires a considerable expansion in simultaneous human activity.

**Off-Earth product sold at a profit — higher.** Conditional on lunar material delivery at scale and recognised resource rights, much of the hard supply-side problem has already been overcome. The remaining written test is only one company, one product, and one profitable financial year. It does not require a large market, repeated profitability, or customers independent of government spending. A government procurement contract could therefore settle it without establishing the unsubsidised economy described in the explanation. The proposed 0.55 appears to price a more demanding economic outcome than the actual criterion.

**Recession recovery — resolved under the intended interpretation.** The historical evidence above satisfies a recovery floor using a reasonable, explicitly stated budget measure. Future catastrophic recessions cannot undo an event defined to resolve at the first successful recovery. Leaving a 10–20% failure probability on this historical milestone confuses “has recovered once” with “will remain resilient.” A future-resilience replacement needs a fresh forecast.

**Permanent residents — higher.** Given that long-term off-Earth living is biologically possible, the existing test asks for only one site and one person, not a general right to immigrate or a large permanent population. I expect substantial variation in operators’ policies, with some allowing founders, researchers, or settlers to remain indefinitely. Universal work-camp rules persisting across every qualifying site seem less likely than the packet assumes. Infrastructure, resources, personal willingness, and the three-year observation period keep this well below certainty.

**C2 — lower.** C1 can be satisfied by a vast computational proof that gives humans little transferable insight. Its resolution therefore provides much weaker support for C2 than “a non-human mind produces knowledge beyond us” suggests. C2 additionally demands identifiable machine origin and adoption as standard scientific practice. Humans may use machine outputs extensively while the methods they teach remain human-designed, or while authorship is too mixed to meet the provenance test. I still expect eventual success to be likely, but 0.80 by 2071 overstates what follows from the actual C1 condition.

C1’s status correction would raise its probabilities to 1.00 throughout, but its largest change is only 0.08, so it is excluded from the numerical table under your 0.15 rule.
