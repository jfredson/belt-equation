## Prompt

> Without looking anything up, give your probability that a specific person holds each of the following, with a one-paragraph reasoning chain for each. The person: a man born in January 1986, healthy, currently in an elite, physically demanding military career that requires top physical and mental condition as a condition of the job, an endurance athlete who trains and performs well above the average for his age, and who puts a deliberate share of his time and income into the lifestyle and medical choices most likely to improve his own health trajectory. Assume no civilization-ending catastrophe. (1) Alive on 2071-01-01, aged 85. (2) Alive on 2080-01-01, aged 94. (3) Alive on 2095-01-01, aged 109. (4) Alive on 2136-01-01, aged 150. (5) Alive and medically fit for a gentle orbital flight with ordinary assistance on each of those four dates. Give the numbers as a table first, then the chains, and say which parts of the description moved your numbers and by how much relative to an average man of the same birth year.

## Astra's reasoning chains (verbatim)

**1. Alive at approximately 85: 70%; alive and flight-fit: 54%.** His current health and unusually strong physical performance are favorable evidence about his underlying health, while sustained exercise and effective preventive care should further improve his prospects. The job's mental and physical requirements provide additional evidence of current functioning, although much of that overlaps with "healthy endurance athlete" and should not be counted twice. Those advantages cannot eliminate cancer, neurological disease, accidents, or unfavorable inherited risks. His military career adds an uncertain occupational hazard, depending heavily on what he actually does and how long he continues. Altogether, I put survival substantially above the average man's 45%. Among his survivors, I assign roughly a 77% chance of flight fitness, producing the 54% joint estimate.

**2. Alive at approximately 94: 28%; alive and flight-fit: 15%.** His advantages still matter, particularly because reaching old age with greater cardiovascular and functional reserve gives him more room to withstand illness and decline. But extending survival into the mid-90s involves more than maintaining excellent fitness: competing causes of death accumulate, and inherited susceptibility and chance become increasingly consequential. I therefore would not simply extrapolate his present performance into exceptional longevity. This estimate includes some continued improvement in prevention and treatment, without assuming a successful general treatment for aging. Of those who survive, I give about 54% a sufficiently favorable combination of cardiovascular, neurological, and musculoskeletal function for the specified flight.

**3. Alive at approximately 109: 0.8%; alive and flight-fit: 0.35%.** This is where the forecast becomes especially sensitive to future medicine. Excellent fitness around age 40 provides only limited evidence that someone will become an exceptionally old survivor, and current lifestyle advantages alone would not justify a large probability. My estimate combines a very small chance of exceptional survival under broadly familiar medicine with an additional chance that effective treatments developed during his lifetime materially extend survival. His willingness and ability to pursue useful care increases the second component, although spending more does not necessarily mean receiving more effective care. The flight estimate assumes a mixture of frail exceptional survivors and people whose survival was extended alongside substantial preservation of function.

**4. Alive at approximately 150: 0.2%; alive and flight-fit: 0.15%.** I would put survival to 150 effectively at zero under medicine that merely improves today's disease-by-disease approach. Almost all this probability comes from a speculative sequence: he survives long enough to encounter major advances against aging, those advances work in humans, he obtains them, and their benefits last or are supplemented by further advances. No civilization-ending catastrophe does not guarantee any of those steps. His profile improves his prospects of surviving to benefit from such developments and pursuing effective treatment, but it does not establish that the necessary science will happen. Flight fitness conditional on survival is higher here, 75%, because most of my surviving scenarios involve substantial restoration or preservation of function, rather than ordinary aging stretched to 150.
