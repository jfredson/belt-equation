# The Belt Equation, kickoff outline

Drafted 2026-09-08 under the working title "Expanse Ledger"; the project was named The Belt Equation the same evening. The structure below is unchanged. Decisions made after this outline live in docs/definitions.md.

## What this is

A long-horizon prediction project. The question it tracks: what is the probability that John becomes a Belter, meaning that a solar system recognizably like the one in The Expanse exists and he gets to take part in it, before his expected date of natural death? The answer is computed from a dependency graph of breakthroughs (a tech tree), each with its own probability, rather than guessed at the top. The graph gets reviewed on a fixed cadence, every revision is logged with an absolute date, and the whole thing is published so other people can argue with the nodes.

Three branches feed the top-level number: technology, economics and politics, and longevity and biology. The third branch is special because it moves the deadline itself, so its nodes act as multipliers on everything else.

## The minimum-viable-presentable version

Five things, and nothing more, are needed before this can go in front of other people:

A definitions document that fixes what "Expanse-like" means, in tiers, and fixes the mortality window with its assumptions stated.

A version-zero tech tree of roughly 40 to 60 nodes with dependencies, a first-pass probability per node, and a one-line rationale per node.

A short methodology note explaining how the tier probabilities are computed from the node probabilities and how the project scores itself over time.

A single visual of the tree with the current numbers on it.

An introductory post that presents the tree, gives the current headline numbers, and asks readers which nodes are missing and which probabilities they'd move.

Everything else (a live site, automated resolution, a database) is version one and can wait until the first round of public critique has changed the tree.

## Step 1. Definitions (target: done by 2026-09-21)

Define the end-state tiers. Each tier gets its own probability, because "Expanse-like" means different things to different people and the disagreement is part of the discussion. A first cut:

Tier 1, Outpost. Permanent crewed presence on the Moon and Mars, hundreds of people, fully supplied from Earth. Antarctica with worse weather.

Tier 2, Settlement. Thousands living off Earth, at least one human born and raised there, partial local self-sufficiency in air, water, and food.

Tier 3, Slow Expanse. Tens of thousands across the Moon, Mars, and at least one Belt or outer-moon site. Transit times in months. A resource economy where something mined off Earth is sold at a profit. Nuclear propulsion in routine use.

Tier 4, Full Expanse. Millions off Earth, transit times in weeks via constant-acceleration drives, a Belt population with its own political identity and economy.

Define the window. Start from an actuarial baseline for a healthy 40-year-old male born January 1986, adjust with the health record (the Health folder is the source of truth), and state the result as a date with a plus-or-minus. Then define two or three longevity scenarios (baseline, moderate extension, strong extension) so the whole tree can be re-run against each. The window itself becomes a node in the longevity branch.

Define resolution. For each node, what observable event counts as "resolved yes," and what source settles it. Without this the annual review becomes an argument with past-self.

## Step 2. Tech tree version zero (target: done by 2026-10-12)

Node schema, one record per node:

- id (short, stable, e.g. TECH-LAUNCH-03)
- name
- branch (technology, economics-politics, longevity-biology)
- description, two or three sentences
- resolution criterion and source of truth
- dependencies (ids of nodes that must resolve first)
- feeds (which tiers this node is required for, or how much it advances each)
- horizon class: leaf (resolvable within 1 to 5 years), mid (5 to 20 years), root (window-scale)
- probability of resolving before the window, with the date of the estimate
- one-line rationale
- status (open, resolved yes, resolved no, superseded)
- revision log (date, old probability, new probability, why)

Branch seeds, to be expanded in a working session:

Technology: reusable heavy launch cadence (flights per year, cost per kilogram), lunar mass driver first test and first operational launch, orbital manufacturing, in-situ resource extraction at industrial scale, autonomous robotic construction without on-site humans, closed-loop life support, radiation shielding (superconducting magnetic shielding as one route), high-temperature superconductors at practical cost, fusion net electricity, fusion propulsion at usable specific power, nuclear-electric and nuclear-thermal propulsion in service, artificial-gravity habitats, a wildcard node for "an enabling breakthrough not currently conceivable."

Economics and politics: a resource extracted off Earth and sold at a profit, orbital or lunar industry with a commercial customer (the AI compute satellite plan is the current candidate), sustained public or private funding through at least one economic downturn, an international or treaty framework for off-Earth property and settlement, absence of a conflict that halts space activity, a stated reason for a million people to live off Earth.

Longevity and biology: mammalian gestation and development in partial gravity, human long-duration health in partial gravity, radiation exposure limits for multi-year missions, the mortality window itself, and the longevity scenarios that move it.

Rule for granularity: at least a third of the nodes should be leaves that could resolve within five years. Those are the ones that make the project fun to track and that tell you early whether the roots are on schedule.

## Step 3. Scoring model (target: done by 2026-10-26)

The headline numbers are computed, not chosen. Simplest workable version: store the nodes as a data file, define each tier as a set of required nodes, and run a Monte Carlo that samples each node's resolution according to its probability while respecting dependencies (a node cannot resolve unless its dependencies did). The fraction of runs in which a tier's required set resolves is that tier's probability. A hundred lines of Python.

Calibration: leaf nodes resolve fast enough to score. Track a Brier score (a standard measure of how well probabilities match outcomes) across resolved leaves each year. Where a public forecasting site has an equivalent question, note its number beside yours as a reference, not an authority.

## Step 4. Tooling and record-keeping (in parallel with steps 2 and 3)

A repo under ~/Code, with docs/ holding this plan and the definitions document, data/ holding the node file, scripts/ holding the Monte Carlo, and a changelog. This kickoff outline is the first file in docs/.

A TimeAssembler project mirroring the plan, with the review cadence as recurring tasks.

Every dated entry uses absolute dates.

## Step 5. Presentation (target: first public post by 2026-11-16)

The visual: a dependency graph with nodes colored by branch and labeled with current probability. Static image is fine for version zero.

The post: a Sentient Horizons essay that presents the tiers, the headline numbers, the three or four nodes that dominate the result, and an explicit invitation to disagree. Reddit companions (r/slatestarcodex, r/TheExpanse, r/space) framed as discussion, with the essay as optional further reading. The ask to readers is concrete: name a missing node, or name a probability you'd move and why.

Collect the responses and fold them into version one before the first annual review.

## Cadence after launch

Quarterly, a fifteen-minute scan for leaf nodes that resolved or moved, logged.

Annually, a full review: every node re-estimated, the Monte Carlo re-run, the Brier score updated, a public changelog post. Suggested anchor: each January, so the review lands near the start of a new year of the window.
